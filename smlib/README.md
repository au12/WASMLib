# SMLib in WASMLib

This is SMLib and SM_API from NVIDIA's [usd-brep](https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/usd-brep) 1.0.1 (commit
3fd5b95, Apache-2.0 — `LICENSE`, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), with the layout kept: `source/SMLib`, `source/SM_API`, and
upstream's own test suite in `tests/SM_API_test`. It was copied in unchanged (WASMLib commit `ae7cce2`); everything
WASMLib has changed since is an ordinary commit, so `git diff ae7cce2 -- smlib/` shows all of it. Each changed file says
so under its licence header, and each change is marked `WASMLib:` in the code.

Every change is for speed and is written so the answers can't change — a test is skipped only where its answer is
known in advance — except the volume change, whose volumes move within the accuracy asked for (below). Upstream's
SM_API test suite must keep passing 127/127 (`tools/build.sh test` in WebAssembly, `tools/build.sh native` natively).

The cases were measured on real calls from [DadCAD](https://github.com/au12/DadCAD)'s benchmark parts, replayed
natively (`g++ -O2`, minimum of several runs) and profiled with callgrind.

| Change | What | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface derivatives** (`NL_SrfNurb.cpp`) | `N_SrfDerivs` works out a NURBS surface's derivatives by summing the homogeneous control net once and applying the quotient rule to the point (The NURBS Book, A4.4) instead of taking the rational derivatives of every one of the (p+1)(q+1) basis functions and then summing those. The same values, a fraction of the work. It's under every curved-surface evaluation with derivatives: cylinders, cones and spheres go through their NURBS form here too. For degrees 1 to 3 each way — nearly every surface — the basis functions, their derivatives and the sum then run as code written for that degree (`wl_BasisDerivs`, `wl_SrfHomogeneousSum`): the same steps in the same order, so the same values to the last bit, without the loops sized for degree 17. | 4 200 → 1 600 instructions a call; a fillet 27 → 21 ms, a volume 16 → 11 ms, a shell 159 → 131 ms. The degree-specific code: a shell 1.10 → 0.99 billion instructions, a fillet 186 → 171 million (built `-O3`, as the WebAssembly is) |
| **Face pairs apart** (`SmTopologyIntersector.cpp`) | A boolean intersected every face of one body with every face of the other — surface/surface intersection and curve classification for each pair. Now each face's cheap bounding box is worked out once, and pairs whose boxes are apart by more than the tolerances are skipped: they can't meet. | joining a knurl's 3 400-facet ring to its knob: 435 456 face intersections → 3 062; one letter cut from a plate 97 → 61 ms |
| **Coplanar edges apart** (`SmFace.cpp`) | When two faces lie in one plane, every edge of both was classified against both faces. Edges whose boxes are apart from the other face's box can't bound where the two overlap, so they're left out. A 126-sided knob end lying in the plane of 126 small facets had each of its edges classified 126 times. | that join: 72 904 curve classifications → 10 760, 3.6 s → 1.2 s natively |
| **Volume only** (`SmApiQueries.cpp`) | `SmApiBrepComputeVolume` integrated every mass property at once — area, volume, first and second moments, products of inertia: 26 numbers at every step — to return one. It now asks each face for area and volume only (the per-face routine has always taken a mask; its step control is area-based either way). | a filleted box's volume 10.7 → 6.4 ms. Volumes move in the 6th–7th significant figure, well inside the accuracy asked for: a filleted box (exactly 47 109.079 mm³) 47 109.159 → 47 109.132; a Ø30 × 15 cylinder (exactly 10 602.875) 10 602.879 → 10 603.020 |
| **Flat-face loops apart** (`SmLoop.cpp`) | Classifying a point on a face marched round every loop of the face. On a flat face, a point outside a loop's UV box (by more than the tolerance) is outside that loop, so the march is skipped. A plate with many letters or holes cut into it otherwise marched round all of them for every point. | cutting the 5th, 10th and 11th letters: 54 → 32, 79 → 39, 201 → 72 ms |
| **Shell pieces** (`SmBrep.cpp`) | Deleting a face or a wire edge from a shell asked whether what's left still hangs together by walking the whole shell from one vertex (`ShellTraversal`) — and a boolean deletes thousands of faces and edges from one big shell, so the walks added up to the square of its size. Now a search goes out from all the vertices in question at once, a vertex each in turn (`wl_ShellPieces`), and stops as soon as the searches meet (still one piece: nothing to do) or one runs out (it's a piece on its own, and the smaller one). When the shell does split, only the smaller piece is walked and moved to the new shell — the same two pieces as before, though when the smaller is on the edge's first end the old shell object now keeps the other one. The full walk is still used where the old code needs its lists: a wire shell whose first edgeuse is the one deleted, or a bigger piece that may have no face. | joining a knurl's 3 400-facet ring to its knob: deleting the faces the boolean throws away 2.16 → 0.08 billion instructions |
| **Closure test** (`SmBrep.cpp`) | As a boolean builds its result face by face, each new face asked whether it closed off a region by walking out from it until the walk came round to the face's other side (`ClosureTraversal`) — most of the open shell, every time. Now a search goes out from both sides of the face at once (`wl_FaceuseReachesMate`); when they meet, the face doesn't close anything off and the walk is skipped. When either search runs out the face may close a region, and `ClosureTraversal` decides as before. | that join: building the result faces 772 → 172 million instructions |
| **Line against line** (`SmCurveClass.cpp`) | Classifying a line on a face intersected it with every edge of the face; for two lines `SmLine::IntersectWithLine` first compares their boxes, which rejects nearly all of them. That box test now runs before the call, with the classification line's end points worked out, and its box grown by the tolerance, once rather than for every edge. | that join: 393 689 line pairs, 26 508 of them near enough to intersect; edge intersection 646 → 393 million instructions (with **Points on lines**) |
| **Vertices far from a line** (`SmCurveClass.cpp`) | Each vertex of the face was then dropped onto the line (`SmLine::DropPointFast`), which keeps it only if it lands within tolerance. A vertex further than twice the tolerance outside the line's box can't, so it's skipped. | that join: 290 → 120 million instructions |
| **Loop lists** (`SmLoopuse.cpp`, `SmFaceuse.cpp`, `inc/WlTools.h`) | A loop's edges and vertices, and a faceuse's edgeuses, were gathered with `AddUnique`, which searches the whole list for each new item: the square of the loop's length, for a list that's gathered thousands of times and on a knob end is 126 long. Items are now added straight away and repeats removed once at the end (`wl_KeepFirstOfEach`): the same list, in the same order. | that join: gathering a face's edges and vertices 322 → 108 million instructions, a faceuse's edgeuses 96 → 21 million |
| **Points on lines** (`SmLine.cpp`) | Every point evaluated on a line converts its parameter, and that asked two virtual functions and an out-of-line one whether the line was bounded. Written out in place. | part of the line-against-line figure above |

All together, the knurl's join takes 5.45 → 2.03 billion instructions, 1.27 s → 0.32 s natively; cutting the 11th
letter from a plate 664 → 506 million (71 → 59 ms).

Tried and dropped: a capped search out from one end of a deleted edge before `DeleteEdge`'s full shell walk — on a
knurl the edge's two ends are often in pieces that just split apart, which a search from one end can't show without
walking its whole piece, so it seldom proved anything and cost more (searching from both ends at once, as **Shell
pieces** does, ends as soon as the smaller piece runs out); and building a polyhedron by gluing faces pair by pair —
region tracking makes every glue walk the shell, 3× slower than SMLib's stitch.

To find the next one: save the inputs of a slow call from the real app (`writeBrep`), replay it in a small native
program linked against `build-native`'s `libsmlib.a` and `libsmapi.a`, and profile that under callgrind — guessing from
the source has been wrong more often than right.
